## Definitions and distinctions

- **Phenomenal connectedness:** experienced togetherness between conscious states. Simultaneous states belong to one consciousness; non-simultaneous states are connected when one is experienced as flowing into the other (PDF p. 2).
- **Phenomenal continuity:** overlapping sequences of phenomenal connectedness, contrasted with overlapping sequences of psychological connectedness (PDF pp. 1-2).
- **Stream of consciousness:** a collection whose simultaneous experiences are phenomenally connected and whose non-simultaneous experiences are phenomenally continuous (PDF p. 2).
- **Inseparability thesis:** all experiences in one non-branching stream are co-personal; Gustafsson attributes this thesis to Dainton and Bayne (Section 2, PDF p. 8).
- **Phenomenal-Continuity Criterion:** the proposed necessary-and-sufficient identity criterion for conscious persons described above (Section 2, PDF p. 8).

## Relevance to consciousness interruptions and uploading

**Interpretation:** the argument supplies a counterposition to the inference that surviving a period without experience establishes a psychological-continuity account of identity. A phenomenal-continuity account could also accommodate such survival. Preserved memories alone do not satisfy Gustafsson's proposed criterion (Sections 1-2, PDF pp. 7-8).

The paper does not discuss deep hypothermic circulatory arrest, neuronal depolarisation or mind uploading. It neither validates an uninterrupted biological or causal process as necessary for identity nor determines whether an upload would retain phenomenal continuity. Its non-branching condition and its distinction between experiential and psychological relations must be preserved when applying the argument beyond dreamless sleep.
