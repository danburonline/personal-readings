# Observations concerning the extinction of humanity and exocivilizations

**Source:** Darren J. Dougan, [archived preprint](20260925_depopulation_great_filter.pdf), arXiv:2602.08188v1, 9 February 2026. The eight-page PDF says "Preprint 10 February 2026" on p. 1. Its document metadata gives a different title, "The Great Filter hypothesis -- a new Great Filter?". The visible manuscript title is used here and in the graph. The journal-style header has an `Accepted XXX` placeholder, so publication or peer review is not established by this copy.

## Argument and models

| Source | Author's proposal | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 2.1, p. 2, Equations (1)-(8) | Introduces exponential and logistic population equations. | These equations provide background; they do not determine the later fertility trajectory. |
| Section 2.4, pp. 4-5, Figures 6-7 and Table 4 | Extrapolates total fertility rate (TFR) towards 1.35 in the 2200s, then projects a world population of 10,000 in 3002. | Conditional model output. The regression specification and long-range uncertainty are not reported. |
| Section 2.4, p. 5, Figure 8 and Table 5 | Under an alternative long-term TFR of 0.6, projects 10,000 people in 2434. | A more extreme scenario, not an observed trend or an extinction date. |
| Sections 3.1-3.2, pp. 6-7 | Proposes that declining fertility could affect exocivilisations and act as a Great Filter. | Analogy from human demography; no exocivilisation fertility data are presented. |
| Section 4, p. 7 | Suggests that machines might remain non-conscious tools and cease operating after human extinction. | Speculation; the paper gives no test of machine consciousness or autonomous expansion. |

## Reading checks

- The text on p. 2 says TFR has fallen "15% each year" since 2016. The decline shown in Figure 1 is much smaller than repeated annual declines of that size. This numerical statement needs checking before reuse.
- Equation (8), p. 2, prints `n(t) = n_0 e^(-gt)` after defining `g = b - d < 0` for population decline. With that definition, the printed sign would imply growth. This appears internally inconsistent.
- The document has link annotations but no visible reader highlights or margin comments in the supplied copy.
